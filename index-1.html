Lessons & concepts

Short study notes organized around the six course handouts and the practical activities provided.

Course topics

HANDOUT 01

Tourism Industry & Information Technology

How information technology supports tourism operations, information flow, and service delivery.

Review lesson
  • Tourism connects travelers with transport, accommodation, attractions, and supporting services.
  • Information technology supports communication, information access, reservations, and coordination.
  • Digital systems help tourism businesses manage information and serve travelers.
HANDOUT 02

ICT in Tourism

Technology and information systems used across tourism businesses.

Review lesson
  • ICT includes tools and systems used to create, store, process, and share information.
  • Tourism applications include online information, reservation platforms, digital payments, and customer communication.
  • Information accuracy, availability, and responsible handling matter in service operations.
HANDOUT 03

E-Commerce Concepts & Issues

Electronic transactions, digital channels, and common considerations.

Review lesson
  • E-commerce involves buying or selling goods and services through electronic networks.
  • Common considerations include privacy, security, payment reliability, access, and customer trust.
  • Online travel services can let customers search, compare, reserve, and pay electronically.
HANDOUT 04

Functional System for the Airline Industry / GDS

How reservation distribution systems make travel content available to sellers.

Review lesson
  • A Global Distribution System (GDS) connects travel content providers with travel sellers and intermediaries.
  • The Lab 5 exercise describes Amadeus as a GDS displaying travel offers and related services.
  • Airline content can be searched through availability, schedule, and timetable displays.
HANDOUT 05

Amadeus Air

Flight availability, schedule and timetable entries.

Review lesson
  • AN is used in your activity for neutral availability displays.
  • SN is used for neutral schedule displays.
  • TN is used for timetable displays.
  • Entries can include city pairs, dates, airline or booking-class qualifiers, and other options as shown in your worksheet.
HANDOUT 06

Passenger Name Record (PNR)

Passenger identity and itinerary elements used in the Lab 6 activity.

Review lesson
  • Your Lab 6 worksheet asks you to create a PNR with passenger name, flight segment, contact, received-from, ticketing, and end-transaction elements.
  • The worksheet uses entries such as NM, SS, AP, APE, RF, TKTL, and ER.
  • After saving and redisplaying, record the locator returned by the system.

PRACTICE METHOD

Use the guide alongside Amadeus

Repeat the task from a blank practice session, then compare the screen response with the worksheet.

Suggested practice sequence
  1. Read the scenario and identify origin, destination, date, and any qualifiers.
  2. Write the entry before typing it.
  3. Enter it in your authorized training environment.
  4. Check that the display matches the requested route and constraints.
  5. For PNR tasks, confirm each element is associated with the intended passenger or segment.
  6. Follow the instructor's end-transaction and verification instructions.
